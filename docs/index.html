Prop cheat sheet · Week 9 · Thu Nov 5 – Sun Nov 8

Week 9 top 47 props

Every Kalshi yardage and receptions ladder across 7 games, scored for how likely it is to hit and whether the price still pays. One best line per player and stat, two props max per player.

How the score works
  • Sleeper projection (41%, started at 28%). Sleeper's Week 9 projection turned into a chance of clearing the line.
  • Player hit rate (14%, started at 20%). His games over this line this season, plus last season's games at a quarter weight each (all together worth at most 3 games), so one fluky game can't swing it.
  • Usage model (shares the projection weight). His recent share of the team's targets or carries times the team's volume per game, times his catch rate and yards per touch (shrunk toward a normal rate so one long play doesn't inflate it). Recent weeks count more. Blended 50/50 with the Sleeper projection.
  • Defense vs line (17%, started at 20%). How many of the opponent's 3 games the same role went over this exact line. Roles are by team depth: QB, lead RB, WR1, WR2, TE1, so a WR2 is compared to WR2s.
  • Price value (17%). The model's chance against the Kalshi price.
  • Defense rank (6%, started at 10%). Where the defense ranks among 32 teams in pass or rush yards allowed per game.
  • Agreement (5%). A small bonus when the projection, his history and the defense all say yes.
  • Safety penalties. Each game he missed the line costs 8%, each defense miss 5%, and thin markets (wide bid/ask) 10%.
  • Cushion (part of hit rate). Not just whether he cleared the line, but by how much. Clearing 15+ with 60 counts far more than clearing it with 16. Recent games count more.
  • Defense matchup + game script (up to ±14% together). How much this defense gives up to his exact role (WR2s, TE1s...) compared with the league average, plus its overall rank. When a soft defense and a game script that forces volume line up (say, a big underdog throwing against a bad pass defense) the two stack.
  • Depth & volume (up to −10%). WR3s, TE2s and backup RBs start with a penalty; expected targets or carries from the usage model can cancel it or make it worse.
  • Offense (up to ±12%). How well his offense moves the ball through the air or on the ground. A QB in his first season with the team, or first season starting, on a struggling offense gets an extra cut.
  • Picking the line. On each ladder the board starts from the safest line, then steps up to a higher one when the case is strong (good matchup and game script, or a projection far above the line), as long as the higher line is still very likely (model 72%+), fairly priced, and scores within a few points of the safest. Each card also shows the Safer line it stepped up from and a Bigger payout line, both addable to your slip.
  • Market check. The final chance is 40% model and 60% Kalshi's own price (Kalshi beat the model on its own in Weeks 4, 5 and 6), minus a 2-point safety haircut, so the board errs on the side of underconfident. Rushing, passing and tight-end props keep only part of any edge over Kalshi.
  • Breaking news (Google News, checked every update). If a headline in the last day and a half says a player's role is being cut (lighter workload, split, benched, inactive, ruled out), he's taken off the board. Injury headlines cost 12% and lean on Kalshi. If his QB is playing hurt or a backup QB is starting, his catch and yardage props lose 10% and stay out of Safe and Medium parlays.
  • Risky prop types. Tight-end catches/yards −10%, running back rushing −15%, QB rushing −10% (over Weeks 4–6 these hit 8–12 points below what the model expected). At most 4 props per team, so one stalled offense can't sink the board.
  • Game script against him (up to −30%). When the spread and total say the game will go the wrong way for this prop (a trailing team's running back, a big favorite's passer, a low-scoring team), the score drops and the chance is cut. Props in that spot hit 46% in Weeks 4–5 when the model expected 80%.
  • Game script (shown separately). From the point spread and over/under (now combined with the defense matchup above). A big favorite leans on the run with a lead, so its lead back gets a boost and its passing a small cut; a big underdog throws to catch up, so its QB, receivers and pass-catching backs get a boost and its rushing a cut. A high or low team total nudges every yardage prop. Starts at a 3-point spread, full strength at 10.
  • QB situation (receivers, up to ±10%). If the QB who usually starts is out or not projected to start, the receiver's games with the new QB decide it (this season, and last season if they were teammates). No games together is a clear penalty; good history with the backup cancels it.
  • Game context (up to ±4%). A small nudge from things the stats can't see: wind and rain at kickoff, injuries on his team and the defense, his own injury status, recent ESPN headlines, and hype (most-added on Sleeper, Reddit buzz). Players listed out are dropped. It's kept small on purpose while it's tested. This run: lines 7 games; weather 5 outdoor games; injuries 142 players listed; news 4 players in headlines; google news 3 role-cut, 48 injury headlines; QB hurt: CLE, MIN, TB; sleeper trending 34 board players trending; reddit unavailable.
  • Kalshi %. The Kalshi yes price is the market's chance it hits; the number under it is the same price as sportsbook odds, before Kalshi's fee.
  • It learns. After each week the picks are graded and these weights and the model's chances are adjusted. See the Results tab.
  • Only lines priced 35¢ to 88¢ are eligible; anything pricier pays too little. Touchdown props are left out because they're too volatile to call safe.
